THE PROBLEM

THE VOTE — TOO SLOW

Governance proves the exploit, then votes to pause.

A public vote needs time to form. Hours of exposure while a live exploit drains the vault. Slow by design — and while it deliberates, the money is gone.

THE ADMIN KEY — TOO POWERFUL

A single key can pause the protocol instantly.

Fast — but it is exactly the key a compromise targets. One leaked key and the attacker can pause, unpause, or do far worse than pause. Speed bought with a single point of failure.

FOR LENDING PROTOCOLS ON GENLAYER

Protect your protocol

Interlock is a guardian, not a bank. You keep full ownership of your contract; Interlock holds one role — the ability to pause — and only when validators independently confirm an exploit.

01

Deploy Interlock

Point a fresh Interlock at your contract's address. Its constitution is frozen at deployment — it can only ever pause that one target.

02

Authorize it as guardian

Your vault owner calls one method granting Interlock the pause role. This is recorded on your audit log — the same log validators read.

03

Done

Now consensus watches every operation. On a confirmed exploit the breaker trips and your protocol pauses. Only your governance resumes it.

INTEGRATION GUIDE

Add Interlock to a live vault

First, check one thing. Does your contract already expose a pause method on an owner-swappable guardian slot — like DemoVault's apply_pause + set_guardian? If yes, use Step A. Different shape? Use Step A′.
STEP A — DIRECT DEPLOY

Matching pause + guardian slot

Deploy Interlock pointed straight at your contract:

genvm deploy interlock.py \ --arg target_address=0x…your_vault… \ --arg governance=0x…your_owner…
STEP A′ — ADAPTER

Differently-shaped contract

Deploy a small adapter next to your contract that exposes one guardian pause, and point Interlock at the adapter. Same chain, no bridge, no relay service:

genvm deploy adapter.py \ --arg target_address=0x…your_vault… \ --arg pause_selector="pause(uint256)"
STEP B — AUTHORIZE AS GUARDIAN

Grant the pause role

From the vault owner account, arm the breaker (owner-only, only while the vault is live — it is written to the audit log):

set_guardian(0x…interlock_address…)
STEP C — DONE

✓ Interlock is now your protocol's circuit breaker: it can pause on confirmed exploit — and it is structurally incapable of doing anything else.
